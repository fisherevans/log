# 2026-09-29 - Pages become load-more; the pager stops counting entries

## What changed

- With JS, `/posts` is one growing stream. The older page appends when the
  reader is within a screen of the bottom (or on a "Load older · page 2 of 3"
  button); from page 2 on, a "Load newer" control at the top prepends the previous
  page and compensates the scroll so what you were reading stays put. At the end:
  "That is everything."
- The static pages `/posts/`, `/posts/page/N/` still exist and are what the JS
  fetches - so no-JS, crawlers and deep links (the permalink back-link fallback
  points at the page an entry sits on) all keep working. Each appended page is a
  `.page-chunk` carrying its URL; leaving from an entry inside one remembers *that*
  page, so the return renders it rather than the page you started on.
- The pager reads "page 1 of 3" instead of an entry range. A page is 20 blocks and a
  burst is one block, so "1-122 of 152" was true and meaningless.
- The stream script now delegates every control click at the document (bursts,
  Show more, Link/Share, the lightbox) and runs its per-entry setup through one
  `enhance(root)`, so an appended page behaves exactly like the first.

## Why

Folding made fixed-size pages incoherent: 20 blocks was 122 entries on page 1 and
30 on page 2, and the pager range said so out loud. Rather than choose a page unit
that lies less, the pages became chunks of one stream - which is also what
deep-linking wanted: land on the chunk that holds the entry, extend in either
direction on demand, never load the whole history to reach 2013.

## Not done

The year rail still links to `/posts/YYYY/` pages (unpaginated, no load-more);
that is fine while a year is at most a few hundred blocks.
